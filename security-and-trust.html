Security & Trust

How We Protect Your Information

Heavy Duty Security Co. runs its own security program the way it advises clients to run theirs: risk-based, documented, and honest about where it stands.

Our Approach

HDSC's security program is organized around the NIST Cybersecurity Framework and draws its control language from NIST SP 800-53 and NIST SP 800-171, with CIS Critical Security Controls and ISO/IEC 27001 used as practical references. Controls are chosen by risk, assigned to named owners, and verified with evidence rather than assumed. Zero Trust principles guide how we grant access: no user, device, or network location is trusted by default.

Core Practices

These are the practices our program is built around.

Identity & Access

  • Multi-factor authentication for workforce and administrative access
  • Least-privilege, role-based access with periodic review
  • Prompt removal of access when roles change or end

Systems & Data

  • Encryption for data in transit and at rest
  • Hardened baseline configurations and timely patching
  • Tiered data classification so sensitive data gets stronger protection

Detection & Response

  • Centralized logging and monitoring
  • Documented incident response with defined severity levels
  • Tested backups and recovery planning for critical systems

People & Partners

  • Security awareness training, including phishing resilience
  • Risk-tiered review of vendors and subcontractors
  • Flow-down of security requirements to partners who touch sensitive work

Protecting Government Information

For federal work involving Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI), our program is designed around NIST SP 800-171 and the requirements of the CMMC program. Our approach is to keep CUI in a dedicated, access-restricted environment separate from general business systems. HDSC does not currently claim a CMMC certification, FedRAMP authorization, or ISO/IEC 27001 certification; where a contract requires one, we will pursue and state it only once it is independently validated.

Responsible Disclosure

If you believe you have found a security vulnerability in one of our websites or repositories, please report it privately rather than publicly. Use the “Report a vulnerability” option on the Security tab of the relevant GitHub repository, or contact HDSC through the business contact details on this site and mark the message “Security.” Please include enough detail to reproduce the issue and avoid accessing data that is not your own. We will acknowledge good-faith reports and work to resolve valid findings promptly.

A Note on Honesty
